**Poisson bracket**:
$$
\{A,B\}=\frac{\partial A}{\partial p_i}\frac{\partial B}{\partial q_i}-
\frac{\partial B}{\partial p_i}\frac{\partial A}{\partial q_i}.
$$
For arbitrary function $O(t)$,
$$
\frac{dO}{dt}=\{H,O\}+\frac{\partial O}{\partial t}.
$$
**Local or point transformation**,
$$
q_i=f_i(Q_j,t),
$$
to be *invertible*, must have a non-vanishing **Jacobi determinant**,
$$
\det\left(\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial Q_j}\right)\neq0.
$$
Generalized volcities must necessarily be linear in $\dot Q_j$, 
$$
\dot q_i=\dot f_i(Q_j,t)=\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial Q_j}\dot Q_j+\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial t}.
$$
Canonical transformations are those, for which Hamilton equations of motion do hold. They are characterized by the form invariance of the action, up to an arbitrary surface term, that is,
$$
\int_{t_a}^{t_b}dt[p_i\dot q_i-H(p_i,q_i,t)]=
\int_{t_a}^{t_b}dt[P_j\dot Q_j-H(P_j,Q_j,t)]+
\left.F(P_j,Q_j,t)\right|_{t_a}^{t_b}
$$
This leads, by invoking the standard Schwarz integrability condition by which all second derivatives are independent of the order of differentiation, to:
$$
\begin{split}
(P_k,Q_l)=\delta_{kl},\\
(P_k,P_l)=0,\\
(Q_k,Q_l)=0.
\end{split}
$$
where **Lagrange brackets** are defined by
$$
(A,B)=\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial A}\frac{\partial q_i}{\partial B}-
\frac{\partial q_i}{\partial A}\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial B}
$$
and so on and are equivalent to Poisson brackets. Time-dependent coordinate transformations satisfying the above equations are called **symplectic**.

An important class of canonical transformations arises when introducing a **generating function** $F$ that depends explicitly on half old and half new variables, e.g. $F(q_i,Q_j,t)$. One can derive the new Hamiltonian:
$$
H'(P_j,Q_j,t)=H(p_i,q_i,t)+\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F(q_i,P_j,t),
$$
where
$$
F(q_i,P_j,t)=F(q_i,Q_j,t)+P_jQ_j.
$$
Coordinates conjugate to momenta which are time independent are called **cyclic**. One can find the respective transformation using the **Hamilton-Jacobi** equation,
$$
\partial_tF(q_i,P_j,t)=-H\left(\partial_{q_k}F(q_i.P_j,t),q_i,t\right).
$$
